Project specification

Real Estate Specification System: Property + Customer

A practical platform for an Indian real estate business that understands what is being sold, what each customer needs, whether a property suits them, and how ready they are to buy.

Stage: Clickable wireframe (V1 MVP scope)Data: Fictional mock data onlyAudience: Sales, management & tech team
Core principle
A high-budget customer is not automatically an HNI, and an expensive property is not automatically a high-value investment. Property value, customer purchasing capacity and sales priority are kept as separate assessments. The system never merges them into one number.
Revised 19 Nov 2026 — property module
Changes agreed while refining the wireframe after the 17 Nov review: (1) properties are added by describing them to an AI assistant, with a short form beside it; only the essentials are required (3-A, 3-B); (2) the exact map location is optional, nearby places come from Google, and a route check is needed only for everyday places close by (3-C); (3) Legal & documents is optional: approval badges with their documents, plus any other documents (3-D); (4) matching treats an approval claimed without its document as Conditional (6).

1What we are building

More than a listing website. The platform has four jobs:

1 · Collect accurate property specifications

One standard record per property, with fields that change by property type, plus location, legal and pricing evidence.

2 · Evaluate and communicate property value

Price per sq.ft. backed by comparable properties, with neutral wording. No unsupported “bargain” claims.

3 · Understand and qualify customers

Purpose, budget range, requirements, timeline, funding and decision-making (BANT), with consent.

4 · Match and convert

Recommend the right property to the right customer with an explanation, then manage follow-ups to booking.

Three independent assessments per customer

AssessmentQuestion it answersShown as
1. Property fit scoreHow well does a property meet this customer’s requirements?Score out of 100 (e.g. 95/100)
2. Purchase readiness scoreHow ready is the customer to buy?Score out of 100 + lead band
3. HNI verification statusHas high-net-worth status been verified through a consent-based process?A status, never a score

B. Type-specific specifications: the form changes with the type

Residential plot

  • Length × width; road width and road access
  • Corner / non-corner; topography
  • Survey number and subdivision
  • Layout approval details (authority + number as stated)
  • Land-use classification
  • Water, electricity, drainage, road infrastructure

Flat / Apartment

  • BHK configuration
  • Carpet, built-up and super built-up area (if applicable)
  • Floor number, total floors, number of lifts
  • Parking allocation
  • UDS (undivided share of land)
  • Monthly maintenance; possession status and expected date

Independent house / Villa

  • House, villa or row house; land area and built-up area
  • Floors, bedrooms, bathrooms, parking
  • Construction year, quality and condition
  • Private garden / amenities
  • Water and power arrangements
  • Approval and completion documentation

Commercial property

  • Category: office, shop/showroom, warehouse, commercial building, industrial shed
  • Carpet / usable area; floor and frontage; road access
  • Parking; permitted usage
  • Occupancy (vacant, tenanted, owner-occupied)
  • Rental income, lease duration and terms
  • Maintenance and operating expenses

A plot never asks for BHK; a flat never asks for a survey subdivision. Amenities (security, lift, park, power backup, etc.) are common to all types.

Far, well-known places — railway station, airport, college, mall — are advertised with the approximate distance. Which categories need a route check, and which are shown by default, is set in Settings → Nearby place categories. Planned and proposed items appear only under “Coming up — not built yet”, never as existing.

E. Pricing & value specification: evidence-based

Price per sq.ft.

Price per sq.ft. = Total property price ÷ Area in sq.ft.

Spec example: ₹30,00,000 ÷ 1,200 = ₹2,500 per sq.ft. In the wireframe, PROP-1001 is ₹28,00,000 ÷ 1,200 = ₹2,333.

Valuation record

  • Comparables: location, type, area, ₹/sq.ft., asking or transaction, date, source, differences affecting price
  • Price review date; valuation professional’s report, if any
  • Neutral positioning: within / below / above comparable range
  • Estimated transaction costs and all-in cost
Do not call ₹2,500 a bargain just because another seller asks ₹2,600. Compare similar properties in the same micro-market with similar approvals, access, dimensions and condition. Stamp duty and registration percentages in the system are editable assumptions; confirm current rates and the guideline value before quoting.

The system suggests a classification; a sales manager can override it.

HNI assessment (restricted)
Visible to sales managers and admins only. Records voluntarily stated signals: previous investment, portfolio, objective, purchase range, funding readiness, decision process, timeline. Status values: Not determined · Not verified · Potential (indicators recorded) · Consent obtained (verification in progress) · Verified · Customer declined. A ₹30 L buyer may be a serious investor; a ₹5 Cr enquiry may have no immediate purchase capacity.
Ethics & privacy rules
  • No bank statements, tax returns or account balances during lead generation.
  • Financial verification only through a lawful, consent-based process with restricted access.
  • Never award points for name, appearance, occupation, caste, religion or other personal characteristics.
  • Collect only necessary information; explain why; record consent before follow-up.

6Module D — Property matching engine

Step 1 · Hard filters (pass / fail)

  • Property must be Available (not Reserved, On hold or Sold)
  • Property type must match
  • Location must be in the customer’s preferred cities
  • Price ≤ maximum budget × (1 + flexibility)

Any failure excludes the property: “Budget mismatch” if price is the only reason, otherwise “Excluded”.

Step 2 · Weighted fit score

M = 0.30B + 0.25L + 0.20S + 0.15A + 0.10P
  • B Budget: 100 at/below preferred, down to 70 at maximum, 55 within flexibility
  • L Location: 100 in a preferred locality (or no preference), 70 if only the city matches
  • S Size: 100 for an exact match, minus 2 points per 1% difference
  • A Amenities: infrastructure (water, power, drainage, road…) + must-have amenities
  • P Preferences: facing, road width, BHK, parking, possession, occupancy…
Module D — Property matching engine (continued)
Critical legal rule
A property with an approval badge claimed without its document (e.g. “Building approval — document pending”) is labelled Conditional match and is never the top recommendation, however high its score. Clean properties are always ranked above conditional ones.

Labels: Strong match ≥ 80 Good match 65–79 Weak match < 65 Conditional match (approval document pending or size off by more than 20%) Budget mismatch / Excluded. Weights are starting assumptions, to be tuned with site-visit and sales results.

Every customer record holds

  • Lead owner; last contact and next follow-up dates
  • Customer requirements and recommended properties
  • Site visit feedback and interest level
  • Negotiation details (offer vs. counter)
  • Current stage and days in stage; reason lost, if unsuccessful
  • Full activity timeline

Manager views

  • Dashboard: total / qualified leads, site visits, bookings, follow-ups due, leads stuck more than 14 days, priority leads, today’s appointments
  • Pipeline board: drag leads between stages; “Lost” requires a reason
  • Reports: conversion funnel, where leads are stuck, source, purpose, reasons lost, unmet demand, executive performance

Appointments: Teams-style calendar

Day, work week (Mon–Sat), week, month and list views. Site visits, office meetings and phone calls are blocks sized to their duration. Click an empty slot to book. The system warns about time clashes (same executive, or the same property for site visits). Reschedule, cancel (with reason), mark completed (with feedback) or no-show. Site visits stay in sync with the customer record; booking one moves the lead to “Site visit scheduled”.

8How everything works: end to end

  1. Property team lists inventory. Describe each property to the AI assistant (a short type-specific form sits beside it) or use Bulk import. Only the essentials are required; the important nearby places appear on their own. The exact location, photos, approvals, documents and valuation are added any time from the property’s To do list.
  2. Customer submits an enquiry. The 5-step public form asks purpose first, then type and location, budget, requirements and timeline, then contact details with consent.
  3. System saves and assigns. A customer ID is created and the lead goes to the least-loaded sales executive. A follow-up is scheduled for the next day only if consent was given.
  4. Matching runs instantly. Hard filters, then the fit score with plain-language reasons. Properties with an approval document pending are flagged as Conditional.
  5. Executive qualifies the lead. BANT questions, funding and engagement update the readiness score and band. Classification is suggested; HNI stays a separate, restricted status.
  6. Recommend and visit. The executive recommends best-fit properties and books site visits in the calendar. Feedback and interest are recorded after each visit.
  7. Negotiate and close. Offer and counter are tracked through to Booking / Closed, or Lost with a reason.
  8. Managers monitor and tune. Dashboard and reports show where leads get stuck. Weights, points and thresholds are adjusted in Settings using real results.
