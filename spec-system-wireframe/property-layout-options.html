Making the AI assistant the main focus

Three ways to lay out the property page so that people create a property by talking to the AI, and the form only supports it — filled in automatically, open when someone wants to check or correct a detail. Same sample property in each: a 2 BHK apartment in Porur, 57% complete.

A · Chat + live summary

The chat takes the wide main column. Beside it, a live summary card shows what the AI has understood, the completion ring, what is still missing and quick buttons for photos and documents. The full form slides out as a drawer — try Review all details.

Role of the form

  • A drawer, closed by default
  • Opened from “Review all details” or from any missing item
  • Every field the AI filled shows an AI tag

Good for

  • AI is clearly the main way in
  • People still see, at a glance, what was understood
  • Smallest change from today’s working pieces

Watch out

  • The summary must stay short — key facts only, not every field
  • On phones the summary moves under the chat

B · Centred chat

A full-width, ChatGPT-style conversation. After each answer the AI shows a compact card of what it picked up, right inside the chat. The form, photos and documents sit behind buttons under the input and open as drawers — try Review details.

Role of the form

  • Hidden — a drawer behind “Review details”
  • The fact card in the chat is the main view of what was understood

Good for

  • The most “AI-first” feel — matches the CTO’s “no forms” idea most closely
  • Very simple screen; works well on phones

Watch out

  • Harder to see the whole property at a glance — facts are spread through the conversation
  • Editing an existing property relies more on the drawer
